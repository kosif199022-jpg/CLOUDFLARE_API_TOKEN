# Smart Thinking (Bryan Greetham) — partial read

Coverage: introduction, ch.1 "How good are we at thinking?", ch.2 "What is smart thinking?", ch.3 conceptual thinking, start of ch.4 (≈65 of 310 pages) + full table of contents (3 stages: conceptual thinking, creative thinking, making decisions).

## Transferred
- We are mostly routine thinkers; we struggle with statistics, logic and risk; not all intuitions are unreliable.
- **Narrative fallacy**: we force events into familiar stories.
- **Five simplifying heuristics** (substituting an easy question for a hard one): representativeness (incl. base-rate neglect and the Linda **conjunction fallacy**), belief bias, anchoring, availability, affect heuristic.
- Smart thinking = **metacognition** (monitor your thinking while you think — especially when intuitions are unreliable and decisions complex) + **counterintuitive thinking** (naive questions, stepping outside normal structures).
- Conceptual thinking: define the question first; the quality of thinking depends on the connections made between ideas; analyse concepts into constituents, synthesise disparate ideas.
- Later stages (creative thinking, analogies, evaluating solutions, risk under uncertainty) are known from the TOC only in this pass.
